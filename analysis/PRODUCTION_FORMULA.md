# Reusable production formula

A practical formula abstracted from the reference’s broad production choices, without reusing its protected story expression:

1. **Choose a compact legend premise.** Use one central viewpoint, a clear threat or mystery, and one emotional question that can be resolved in roughly 2 minutes.
2. **Write a narration-first script.** Establish the premise in the opening 10–15 seconds, then use short clauses that each add one fact, consequence, or question. Let images reinforce rather than merely duplicate every line.
3. **Map narration to 10–15 visual beats.** Give each beat a single readable subject/action and a location cue. Use approximate screen time based on sentence length; hold key images long enough for mobile reading.
4. **Board as economical tableaux.** Favor locked wide compositions and simple foreground/background separation. Reserve closer framing for an important clue, injury, decision, or final reaction. Decide where a pose swap or hard cut is clearer than continuous action.
5. **Build an original visual kit.** Define a restricted night/day palette, silhouette rules, facial feature scale, outline weight, recurring props, and sparse environment shapes. Design fresh characters, symbols, costumes, and locations for every story.
6. **Animate selectively.** Hold most drawings; animate only story-bearing changes (gesture, blink, mouth, prop state, water/light loop). Add a modest camera move only when it directs attention or clarifies space.
7. **Design caption-safe compositions.** Keep important faces and props out of the upper text band; use large, high-contrast captions, short line lengths, and verify at phone size.
8. **Shape suspense with visual information.** Show a warning, delay the full reveal, present a consequence, then show a credible action that can change the outcome. Use pauses before key visual changes, not arbitrary darkness.
9. **Pay off the ending.** Resolve the central question, then add a short, meaningful final reaction or image only when it strengthens the theme and does not confuse the causal outcome.
10. **Plan audio deliberately.** Record narration with controlled pacing; add music and effects only to support emotion and action. Mix and check the final track by listening. The reference’s audio content could not be verified in this analysis, so this recommendation is a production requirement rather than an extracted fact.
11. **Quality-control the export.** Check the full runtime, caption spelling, framing, contrast, scene continuity, voice/music balance, and phone playback. Preserve source and intermediate assets separately.

## Reference-derived takeaways

The strongest reusable traits are vertical mobile framing, narration-led exposition, simple symbolic sets, dark/light staging, limited movement, tableau-to-tableau editing, delayed reveals, and expressive close-ups. These are general techniques. The original names, wording, character designs, and particular sequence of events should not be reused.

## Confidence

Visual style and broad narrative progression: medium confidence from full-duration 3-second sampling and scene-change review. Exact cut frames and brief motions: low-to-medium confidence. Audio content and detailed timing: low confidence because the audio was extracted and measured but not auditioned/transcribed.
