# Animation analysis

## Observed

- The 30 fps file presents largely held illustrations: repeated samples show nearly identical layouts over 3-second spans, with occasional changed arm position, facial expression, prop state, or caption.
- Narrative action is commonly conveyed by editing between pose tableaux (e.g., confrontation → dropped object → face in water) rather than visibly continuous body mechanics.
- Several broad scene changes are hard cuts. No sampled evidence establishes a consistent pan, tilt, zoom, or multiplane parallax. Camera framing remains mostly locked.
- A small number of lamp/halo and water-line marks suggest visual emphasis or stylized atmosphere. Actual flicker, wave cycle, or effect animation cannot be resolved from the sparse samples.
- FFmpeg scene-change candidates at 5.23, 15.33, 23.37, 31.67, 35.70, 92.47, and 99.30 s are review cues, not a definitive shot-boundary list.

## Production hypotheses (not verified)

The economy of movement is consistent with limited animation using held drawings, pose swaps, and editorial cuts. The 30 fps export does not prove artwork was animated on ones; the art could instead have been moved on twos, held for longer, or composited as separate layers. Separate character/background layers, rigging, and exact software cannot be established from this MP4.

## Reusable motion plan

For a new short, design a small set of readable key poses per scene, then spend motion only where it changes story information: blink or mouth change, one arm gesture, prop drop, a slow reveal, and a restrained water/light loop. Keep the camera mostly locked; use a motivated crop or push-in for a reveal, and reserve transitions for meaningful location or time changes. Do not imitate the source’s exact character designs or scene sequence.
